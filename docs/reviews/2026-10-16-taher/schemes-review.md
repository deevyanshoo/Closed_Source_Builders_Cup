# PR 115 review: carbon schemes and green finance
Reviewed 2026-10-16. Exact PR 115 head `4abc75b586b274e1d97be5a512d4d3706e9792c2` against PR 114 head `2eb31e730b8f5df1981aed451048d73231b88db1`. Read-only review of the ten-file delta; carbon-factor/plan calculator from PR 114 is outside this review.

**Recommendation: fix before shipping the schemes panel.** The catalogue is useful as an indicative discovery list, but its computed monetary benefits and several programme terms are insufficiently constrained or sourced. If time is tight, ship the official links and eligibility caveats with numerical benefit calculation disabled for the affected entries; do not present them as available savings.

## Findings

1. **P1 — ZED subsidy can exceed the scheme's certification fee; current women-owned rule is misstated.** `procurement-engine/config/carbon_schemes_v1.json:64-72`, `procurement-engine/src/procure/carbon/schemes.py:102-106`, `procurement-engine/tests/test_carbon_schemes.py:68-75`. The app accepts any user-entered “certification cost” and multiplies it by 80/60/50%. The test's small-enterprise example takes ₹250,000 and reports ₹150,000 as a subsidy. The Ministry's 2025-26 report gives ZED certification fees of ₹8,000/₹32,000/₹72,000 for Bronze/Silver/Gold, a ₹10,000 joining reward, and 100% subsidy for women-owned MSMEs. The registry's “additional 10% ... for example women” is therefore outdated; the 10% additional category is for SC/ST and certain locations in that report. This can materially overstate benefits even with “approximate” wording. Model the selected level and official fee, apply the relevant joining reward/subsidy rules, cap the calculation to the qualifying fee, or leave the amount unavailable until those details are known. Source: [Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2025-26](https://msme.gov.in/sites/default/files/MSMEANNUALREPORT2025-26ENGLISH_0.pdf), “MSME Sustainable (ZED) Certification Scheme” (search-index extraction corroborated; the PDF did not render in the browser tool); [Ministry 2025 scheme booklet](https://my.msme.gov.in/files/assets/pdf/v2.0%20-2025-%20Scheme%20booklet%20Eng-14.02.2025.pdf).

2. **P1 — CBAM output is presented as an EU buyer's avoided cost without the eligibility and certificate adjustments needed for that conclusion.** `procurement-engine/config/carbon_schemes_v1.json:100-118`, `procurement-engine/src/procure/carbon/schemes.py:53-58,98-101`, `procurement-engine/tests/test_carbon_schemes.py:45-62`. Matching uses a coarse material ID and destination but no CN code, importer annual mass, import quarter, free-allocation adjustment, or carbon price already paid. With a test steel route and 120 avoided tCO2e it returns €9,878.40 as “approximate saving.” EU guidance says importers above the 50-tonne mass threshold are subject to the authorised-declarant requirement, paid carbon price is deductible, and the number of certificates includes a free-allocation adjustment. The €82.32 default is the Q3 2026 price, applicable to Q3 imports only; route profiles provide no import date, so the app applies it to any period. Keep CBAM as a possible compliance exposure; show price × tonnes as a clearly named gross sensitivity only when the applicable quarter is specified, or withhold a buyer “saving” until the required importer and certificate inputs exist. Sources: [European Commission definitive regime](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en), [price schedule](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/price-cbam-certificates_en), [sector guidance including free-allocation adjustment](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-sectors_en).

3. **P1 — SIDBI is labelled a cheaper loan and given precise programme terms that its cited page does not establish.** `procurement-engine/config/carbon_schemes_v1.json:77-96`, `apps/web/src/demo-schemes.tsx:10,49,53,59`, `procurement-engine/src/procure/carbon/schemes.py:107-115`. The linked SIDBI green-finance landing page confirms products exist, and SIDBI's annual report confirms the GFS was operating, but neither retrieved official page supports the registry's ₹50-crore/75%/10-year/18-month/MCLR claims or an automatic rate concession. The formula accepts an arbitrary “green loan rate you are offered,” then labels `principal × rate difference` a saving; it omits amortisation, fees, tenure and whether the two loans finance the same cash flow. Repro: ₹600,000,000 loan, 20% current and 1% green rates returns ₹114,000,000 per year despite exceeding the registry's stated ₹50-crore limit. An actual SIDBI offer could support a first-year simple-interest comparison, but absent a quote it must not be framed as a scheme saving or a cheaper loan. Obtain an official current GFS term sheet and add amount/project-cost validation, or retain only “green project financing may be available” and mark financial benefit unavailable. Sources: [SIDBI green finance](https://www.sidbi.in/en/green-finance), [SIDBI FY2024-25 business performance](https://www.sidbi.in/annualreport/AnnualReport202425/business-performance.php). The latter confirms GFS and 4E but not these precise terms.

4. **P2 — Carbon-credit arithmetic is potential gross proceeds, not savings or issued/tradeable value.** `procurement-engine/config/carbon_schemes_v1.json:16-27,38-49`, `procurement-engine/src/procure/carbon/schemes.py:98-101`, `apps/web/src/demo-schemes.tsx:53-59`. The offset and compliance entries report user-entered tonnes × user-entered certificate price beneath “Approximate saving,” without proof of project validation, issued certificates, sale, transaction costs, or applicable obligated-entity target. BEE says compliance designation is by government, targets are emission intensity per product output, offset projects go through registration and verification, and BEE issues certificates after the prescribed process. A fictional Indian company can enter 40 t and ₹1,500 and see ₹60,000 even though no credit or market price is evidenced. Relabel as a hypothetical gross certificate-value sensitivity, require an explicit issued-credit count or verified target/output basis, and avoid “tradeable credit” or available-savings language until issuance and market status are known. Source: [BEE Carbon Market](https://beeindia.gov.in/show_content.php?lang=1&level=1&ls_id=116&lid=294), especially compliance/offset and issuance sections.

5. **P2 — A higher green-loan rate is still displayed under “Approximate saving.”** `procurement-engine/src/procure/carbon/schemes.py:107-115`, `apps/web/src/demo-schemes.tsx:53,59`. Existing test `test_a_loan_rate_higher_than_today_is_shown_as_extra_cost_not_saving` proves the backend returns `-10000` for ₹1,000,000 at 9% vs 10%, but the UI always heads the result “Approximate saving” and the scheme remains tagged “Cheaper loan.” Distinguish extra cost, zero difference, and potential interest reduction; do not label a negative number as saving.

## What checked out and what remains unverified

- Matching uses only synthetic company country/sector/routes, returns “possibly eligible, check conditions,” and says an empty registry match does not prove no scheme exists. The API uses the demo session, same-origin and CSRF checks, a context-version guard, and a company-scoped export. No bypass found in this delta.
- The Q3 2026 CBAM price **€82.32 published 5 October** is correctly transcribed from the Commission price table; the defect is applying it without import-quarter context. BEE's nine sectors and one certificate per tCO2e are consistent with its page. SIDBI GFS exists. ZED 80/60/50 category rates are consistent with Ministry material; the calculator's eligible base and women rule are the defects.
- Decimal multiplication and percent division are exact within the accepted six-decimal inputs, but displayed currency amounts are not rounded to INR/EUR minor units. Input rate bounds are absent; e.g. 0–100% is not enforced. This is lower priority than the missing scheme constraints above.
- Offline Python: `test_carbon_schemes.py` and `test_demo_carbon_schemes_app.py` gave **10 passed, 1 failed**. The sole failure, `test_deploy_package_ships_the_scheme_registry`, is a snapshot-preparation limitation: `prepare_package.py` expects `apps/web/dist`, which is absent from the immutable PR snapshot. Excluding that test gives **10 passed**. This does not prove packaging is broken in a built checkout. Frontend Vitest/typecheck were not run because the snapshot has no `apps/web/node_modules` or `dist`; no installation was authorised.
- No product files, Git state, provider, browser, or cloud were changed. The review is source and official-document based; it does not claim hosted behaviour.
