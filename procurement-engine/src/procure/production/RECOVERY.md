# Local receipt-arrival recovery comparison

`compare_receipt_recovery(snapshot, scenario, recovery)` is a pure internal mapping interface. It compares a synthetic original production baseline, the same assignments under explicit replacement receipt dates, and caller-supplied recovery assignments under those dates. It does not choose or apply a schedule, establish an observed disruption, dispatch work, or promise production.

`snapshot` uses the complete public [`screen_production_schedules` schema](README.md). `scenario` has exactly `workspace_id`, `expected_plan_version`, `source_snapshot_id`, `scenario_id`, `synthetic: true`, and `arrival_replacements`. Each replacement has exactly `receipt_id`, `expected_arrival_at`, and `arrival_at`; the list or tuple may be empty. `recovery` has exactly the public alternative fields `workspace_id`, `expected_plan_version`, `source_snapshot_id`, `scenario_id`, `synthetic: true`, and complete `assignments`. Scenario, recovery, and snapshot scope/version/source must match, including `scenario_id` between scenario and recovery. IDs are nonempty trimmed strings. Unknown fields and nonstring mapping keys are unsupported. A receipt may appear once in `arrival_replacements`, even if two proposed rows are identical.

The public production screen is authoritative for the complete original snapshot, recovery assignment, every expected original date, and every replacement date. The wrapper first retains its original baseline, then validates an expected-date copy through the public screen and compares normalized UTC instants. This permits equivalent offset spellings but rejects stale expected instants, malformed or pre-`as_of` dates and unsupported precision. Finally it screens a copy in which **only** targeted `arrival_at` values change. If an original receipt ID has identical duplicate rows, all copies receive the replacement; conflicting duplicates fail in the public screen. Any rejection returns a generic reason with no numeric schedule or caller identifiers. All inputs remain untouched, including read-only mappings and tuple arrays.

A compared result carries `synthetic: true`, source/scope IDs, normalized `as_of`, the public `modeled_checks` and `unmodeled_constraints`, and three unaltered public schedule shapes: `original_baseline`, `disrupted_baseline`, and `recovery`. `receipt_changes` lists actual changed target IDs with UTC before/after instants, sorted by ID; same-instant replacements are omitted. `potentially_exposed_order_ids` includes every declared order whose BOM uses a component of an actually changed receipt. `disruption_changed_order_ids` compares each entire order row in the original and disrupted baselines; `recovery_changed_order_ids` compares each entire order row in disrupted baseline and recovery. Potential exposure does not imply a numerical order-row change. All three schedules retain all orders, maintenance, resource capacity, per-component units, material reservations, due checks, and modeled failures.

Numeric and timestamp bounds are inherited from the public screen: plain decimal input at most 1024 digits, exact bounded arithmetic and decimal serialization, aware times with at most microsecond precision. A material shortage does not release other reserved components or suppress workload/due checks. The comparison has no mixed-unit total or global feasibility flag. Scenario IDs, dates and source references are caller assertions, not verified event provenance, authenticated tenancy, durable deduplication, or physical production state. Setup, work in process, intra-period sequencing, dispatch and customer promises remain unmodeled.
